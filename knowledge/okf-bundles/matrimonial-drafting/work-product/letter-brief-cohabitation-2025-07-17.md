## PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

This matter is before the Court on Defendant’s application to terminate his alimony obligation on the grounds of Plaintiff’s alleged cohabitation. In the alternative, Defendant requests that the Court find that he has met his burden of establishing a prima facie showing of cohabitation warranting discovery and further proceedings.

Importantly, the parties agree on several critical legal principles as follows:

(a) The parties agree that their February 11, 2014 Property Settlement Agreement (“PSA”) is an enforceable, freely bargained for and negotiated settlement.

<!-- Page 2 -->

(b) The parties further agree that definition of “cohabitation” as set forth in their PSA was agreed upon voluntarily, absent any fraud, coercion or duress.

(c) The parties also agree that the PSA was entered prior to the 2014 statutory amendments to N.J.S.A. 2A:34-23. As a result, the statutory definition of “cohabitation” as set forth in N.J.S.A. 2A:34-23, does not apply to the instant matter. See Landers v. Landers, 444 N.J. Super. 315 (App. Div. 2016)(the statute will not supplement the parties’ expressed intent in their negotiated agreement as to cohabitation); see also Cardali v. Cardali, 255 N.J. 107 (2023).

(d) The parties agree that the definition of “cohabitation” as set forth in their PSA, is controlling and governs the circumstances under which alimony may be terminated. Therefore, the parties agree that applying pre-statue case law regarding cohabitation to the instant matter would be inappropriate and would serve to improperly expand the specific and narrow definition of “cohabitation” that they voluntarily and knowingly agreed upon in the PSA. See Quinn v. Quinn, 225 N.J. 34, 50 (2016) citing Konzelman v. Konzelman, 158 N.J. 185, 197 (1999).

(e) Finally, the parties also agree that Defendant must establish, at a minimum, a threshold, prima facie showing of cohabitation as that term is defined by the PSA, in order for this matter to proceed to discovery and potentially a Plenary Hearing. Absent such a prima facie showing, Defendant’s application fails and must be denied.

In the instant matter, Defendant has failed to make the requisite prima facie showing of cohabitation as defined by the explicit terms of the parties’ PSA. As such, no further proceedings or discovery should be permitted and Defendant’s request for relief should be denied.

## STATEMENT OF RELEVANT FACTS

Plaintiff incorporates her Certification dated July 17, 2025 herein and relies upon same as a statement of relevant facts in support of this letter brief.

Briefly, pursuant to the terms of the PSA, Defendant agreed to pay limited durational alimony to Plaintiff in the amount of $8,800.00 per month for a period of fifteen (15) years and then $5,000.00 per month for an additional five (5) years concluding on December 31, 2033. In addition to the alimony obligation, Defendant is required to pay Plaintiff $900.00 per month as a “car allowance” “for as long as spousal support is being paid.”

Further, pursuant to the PSA, Defendant’s alimony obligation shall terminate upon the earliest happening of one of the following events: (a) Plaintiff’s remarriage; (b) death of either party; (c) expiration of the alimony term; or, (d) cohabitation with an unrelated adult, if any, defined by the parties as: the Wife residing full-time with a significant other for a duration of no less than one year. This definition of cohabitation was a carefully crafted, bargained for provision of the PSA. The language is unequivocal and a strict definition of cohabitation. The term “residing full time” refers to living in the same residence on a continuous, daily basis, not on an occasional, temporary, or intermittent basis, and not merely for a “significant amount of time.” Similarly, the phrase “for a duration of no less than one year” indicates that such cohabitation must occur every day, without interruption, for at least twelve (12) consecutive months.

Therefore, in order for Defendant to prove cohabitation under the parties’ PSA, Defendant must prove that Plaintiff has resided with a significant other, on a full-time basis for continuous period of no less than twelve (12) consecutive months.

## LEGAL ARGUMENT: DEFENDANT HAS FAILED TO MEET HIS PRIMA FACIE BURDEN OF ESTABLISHING COHABITATION AS DEFINED BY THE PARTIES’ PROPERTY SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT

Under New Jersey law, a prima facie showing is defined as “evidence that, if unrebutted, would be sufficient to establish a claim.” Quinn v. Quinn, supra at 55; Cardali, supra at 108-109. However, the prima facie burden is not met by speculation, suspicion, or conclusory assertions. It requires a factual foundation that, if accepted as true, would support relief under the governing agreement or statute. See Quinn supra(holding that courts may only order discovery upon a prima facie showing supported by competent evidence); Hand v. Hand, 391 N.J. Super. 102, 105 (App. Div. 2007) (bald assertions of cohabitation are insufficient to meet the prima facie burden); Ozolins v. Ozolins, 308 N.J. Super. 243, 248 (App. Div. 1998) (generalized allegations do not support a claim of cohabitation).

As set forth above, the parties’ PSA provides for a specific, unambiguous definition of “cohabitation:” the Wife residing full-time with a significant other for a duration of no less than one year. Based upon this specific, narrow and controlling definition, in order for Defendant to meet his burden of establishing a prima facie showing of cohabitation, Defendant must present competent, objective evidence supporting each of the following elements:

(1) Evidence that Plaintiff and [Partner] share a primary and permanent residence;

(2) Evidence that Plaintiff and [Partner]’s living arrangements are full-time, continuous, daily overnight presence; and,

(3) Evidence that the full-time co-residency has existed for no less than twelve (12) consecutive months.

<!-- Page 5 -->

Absent competent evidence that speaks directly to each of these elements of the parties specific definition of cohabitation, Defendant fails to meet his burden of establishing prima facie cohabitation.

(a) Defendant’s evidence fails to establish Plaintiff and [Partner] share a residence.

The PSA’s definition of cohabitation imposes upon Defendant a contractually heightened burden to prove a shared primary residence over a sustained period of time. The evidence submitted by Defendant, namely the private investigator’s report, fails to prove that Plaintiff and [Partner] share a common or primary residence.

Defendant even acknowledges that [Partner] owns his own home in [Partner’s Municipality]. [Partner] has his own home, pays his own bills at that home, exercises parenting time with his own children predominately in his [Partner’s Municipality], New Jersey home and absolutely spends significant time overnight at his own home in [Partner’s Municipality].

Defendant has failed to provide any documentary evidence, no postal records, change of address filings, voter registrations, vehicle registrations, no change in tax filings, etc., listing [Partner] at Plaintiff’s address or other such hallmarks of a shared full-time residence.

[Partner]’s drivers license reflects his address at his own home in [Partner’s Municipality]. [Partner] receives his mail at his own home in [Partner’s Municipality]. Defendant has failed to produce any record, lease, deed or any indication that [Partner] contributes to any expenses at Plaintiff’s residence. On the contrary, Plaintiff specifically certified that she and [Partner] pay their own household bills for their separate and independent residences.

Based upon the most liberal review of the evidence submitted by Defendant, it is clear that Defendant has failed to establish, even prima facie, that Plaintiff and [Partner] share a common, permanent residence necessary to meet his burden of proving cohabitation.

<!-- Page 6 -->

(b) Defendant’s evidence fails to establish that Plaintiff and [Partner] reside together on a continuous, full-time basis.

To meet his burden of establishing cohabitation under the terms of the parties’ PSA, Defendant must establish that Plaintiff and [Partner] reside together “full-time.” In other words, the living arrangement between Plaintiff and [Partner] must be continuous, not sporadic or occasional. As New Jersey courts have held, cohabitation claims based on a romantic partner at a home from time to time or periodic overnight stays are insufficient to constitute cohabitation. Harrington v. Harrington, 281 N.J. Super. 39, 47 (App. Div. 1995); Ozolins v. Ozolins, 308 N.J. Super. 243, 248 (App. Div. 1998).

The evidence presented by Defendant fails to establish that Plaintiff and [Partner] reside together on a “full-time” basis.

On the contrary, Defendant’s private investigator conducted surveillance that covered a period of 374 days (February, 2024 to February, 2025), but actual observation occurred on only 208 days. There were 166 days without any surveillance creating significant gaps and meaning 166 days without any confirmed overnights, including zero confirmed overnight stays the entire month of December 2024. Defendant has failed to provide this Court with any proof of cohabitation for nearly one-half of this surveillance period. That is a far cry from establishing full- time, continuous residence as required to prove cohabitation under the PSA.

In addition, Defendant’s “evidence” included confirmation that Plaintiff and [Partner] did not spend overnights together on 29 documented days with no interaction at all on 15 observation days. Even Defendant’s own surveillance confirmed that Plaintiff and [Partner] did not stay overnight together on at least 29 days (not including the 166 days without any confirmation of overnights) and were not even seen together on 15 days of the 374 day “surveillance period.”

<!-- Page 7 -->

Defendant’s own “evidence” undermines the assertion of continuous, full-time residence together meeting the definition of cohabitation under the PSA. On the contrary, Defendant’s evidence established that 210 days (166 without any surveillance + 29 days of documented non- overnights + 15 days of no interaction at all) out of the 374 day “surveillance period,” or more than one-half of the “surveillance period,” Plaintiff and [Partner] were not even together.

Accordingly, Defendant has failed to make any prima facie showing that Plaintiff and [Partner] reside together on a full-time basis as required by the parties’ PSA.

(c) Defendant has failed to establish that Plaintiff and [Partner] have resided together on full-time basis for at least twelve (12) consecutive months.

Finally, to meet the burden of establishing prima facie cohabitation, Defendant bears the burden of establishing, with competent evidence, that Plaintiff and [Partner] resided together continuously and exclusively at the same address for at least 12 consecutive months. This element is not merely a factual footnote, it is a threshold legal requirement agreed to by both parties in the PSA.

Based upon an objective review of the evidence submitted by Defendant, Defendant has failed to meet this burden.

Defendant’s evidence is primarily limited to sporadic surveillance, selective social media content, and anecdotal observations, none of which establish that [Partner] resided in Plaintiff’s home on a full-time basis for any continuous 12-month period.

No calendar record of overnight stays has been produced that even attempts to identify a start and end point for a 12-month period. While the “surveillance period” covered 374 days, the actual observation only covered 208 days, not a full, continuous 12-month period. In addition, there were 166 days of documented non-overnights or no surveillance, creating significant gaps; 29 days were no overnights were observed; and 15 days were no interaction occurred at all.

<!-- Page 8 -->

The surveillance only reveals inconsistent and intermittent visits, not residency and certainly not a sustained period, let alone a full 365 days. On many days, [Partner] is not seen at Plaintiff’s residence at all, be it the 166 days of non-surveillance, the 29 days of documented non-overnights or the 15 days of documented non-interaction between Plaintiff and [Partner].

This lack of documentation and competent evidence is fatal to Defendant’s claim. As the Appellate Division explained in Ozolins v. Ozolins, 308 N.J. Super. 243, 248 (App. Div. 1998), courts cannot infer cohabitation based on mere frequent presence or periodic visitation. The law demands proof of sustained, uninterrupted co-residency and in this case, for no less than one full year.

Therefore, Defendant has failed to make any prima facie showing that Plaintiff and [Partner] reside together on a full-time basis for a continuous period of twelve (12) consecutive month, as required by the parties’ PSA.

## CONCLUSION

This matter is governed by the clear, negotiated terms of the parties’ PSA, which sets forth a narrow and unambiguous definition of cohabitation: full-time residency with a significant other for a continuous period of no less than twelve (12) consecutive months. As such, it is not enough for Defendant to demonstrate the existence of a romantic relationship, periodic companionship or involvement in each other’s lives, families and common social circles. Defendant must present competent, objective evidence establishing uninterrupted, full-time co-residency over a sustained one-year period.

Defendant has failed to do so. Defendant’s application is supported by nothing more than intermittent surveillance, conclusory assertions, and isolated snapshots of social behavior. The evidence, even taken in the light most favorable to Defendant, fails to establish that Plaintiff and [Partner] share a primary residence, that they reside together on a continuous, full-time basis, or

<!-- Page 9 -->

that they have done so for twelve consecutive months, each of which is a required element under the PSA’s strict cohabitation standard.

Indeed, Defendant's own evidence contradicts his claim: the surveillance data confirms long periods of no interaction, including entire months without overnight stays, and there is no documentary proof—such as address records, financial interdependence, or shared household obligations—showing any form of enduring, co-residential life between Plaintiff and [Partner]. These significant evidentiary gaps are fatal to Defendant’s application.

Because Defendant has failed to make a prima facie showing as required under the PSA, Defendant’s application must be denied in its entirety. Discovery or further proceedings are not warranted, and Plaintiff respectfully requests that this Court enforce the PSA, deny Defendant’s request for termination or modification of alimony, and award Plaintiff counsel fees and professional fees incurred in defending against this baseless and overreaching application.
