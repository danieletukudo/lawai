ii\. <u>The Reasonableness and Good Faith of the Positions Advanced by the Parties</u>. Plaintiff's opposition was filed out of necessity due to Defendant's Motion for Reconsideration, which was filed only eight (8) days after entry of the June 11, 2026 Order and does not identify a single ruling decided on a palpably incorrect basis or any evidence overlooked by the Court. The fees incurred in connection with this matter are a direct and foreseeable consequence of Defendant's unreasonable litigation conduct. Plaintiff has acted in good faith throughout these proceedings: she communicated with Defendant substantively through Our Family Wizard on every subject he raised, promptly corrected an initial cost estimate concerning [Child]'s Chinese-school tuition, accommodated three separate short-notice requests to depart from the Court-ordered exchange procedure, and complied with the decision-making authority allocated to her under the December 6, 2024 and June 11, 2026 Orders. Defendant, by contrast, has not advanced his positions in good faith. Defendant's own Certification materially overstated his pre-filing resolution efforts, including a claim that the parties engaged in mediation, a claim the documentary OFW record does not support. Defendant repeatedly conflated his right to receive information concerning [Child] with a veto over decisions committed to Plaintiff's final authority, most notably with respect to [Child]'s enrollment in a Sunday Chinese-language program, despite the Court's express ruling on the record that the decision belongs to Plaintiff. Defendant engaged in a pattern of unilateral self-help regarding his financial obligations, conditioning payment of daycare and extracurricular expenses, and of court-ordered child support arrears, on obtaining separate relief he has not been granted. Defendant sought "legal or professional consequences" against Plaintiff and her counsel without identifying any competent evidence of a false statement, and renewed a demand that Plaintiff submit to a mental health evaluation without identifying any diagnosis, treatment failure, dangerous conduct, or nexus to [Child]'s welfare. Defendant's Short Supplemental Legal Brief also contained an apparent internal drafting instruction left in the filed document, which Defendant did not remove or reconcile with the accompanying Supplemental Certification before filing. Defendant's Supplemental Submissions, filed more than three weeks after his original motion, expanded rather than narrowed the issues before the Court. Accordingly, Defendant should be solely responsible for the full amount of Plaintiff's counsel fees as set forth above.
