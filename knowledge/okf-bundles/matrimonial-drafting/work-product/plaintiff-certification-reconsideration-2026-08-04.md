1.  I am the Plaintiff in the above-captioned matter and the mother of the minor child, [Child] (born [date omitted]). I submit this Certification in opposition to Defendant’s Motion for Reconsideration and Request for Clarification, seeking reconsideration of the Court’s Order entered June 11, 2026 (the “June 11, 2026 Order”) (attached as **Exhibit A**), and in response to the Reconciliation Table, Short Supplemental Legal Brief, and two Supplemental Certifications Defendant filed on July 15, 2026 (collectively, the “Supplemental Submissions”).

2.  Defendant’s application is not a motion for reconsideration. It is a second attempt to obtain relief this Court already denied or resolved, dressed in the language of “implementation” and “clarification.” Defendant does not identify a single ruling the Court decided on a palpably incorrect basis or any evidence the Court overlooked. As set forth below, the documentary record, including Defendant’s own contemporaneous messages, directly contradicts several of the central factual premises of his motion: that he exhausted good-faith resolution efforts before filing, that the June 11, 2026 Order is unworkable, and that his requests reflect anything other than his continuing refusal to accept the Court’s allocation of decision-making authority to me. **<u>DEFENDANT’S MOTION IS A REQUEST FOR A SECOND BITE AT THE APPLE</u>**

3.  Defendant states repeatedly that his motion is “not intended to relitigate every issue previously presented to the Court” and that he “recognizes and appreciates” that the Court resolved custody, parenting time, holiday scheduling, child support, and other matters in the June 11, 2026 Order. Having made that concession, Defendant nonetheless asks the Court to review dozens of pages of background dating to 2022 and thirty-three (33) exhibits, without connecting any of it to a specific request for relief the Court is alleged to have failed to decide. He does not identify a single ruling as having been wrongly decided. He simply re-argues requests already decided against him and asks this Court for another opportunity to litigate them.

4.  To the extent Defendant is asking the Court to conduct a wholesale audit of the June 11, 2026 Order against every position he raised in his prior Cross Application, that is not the function of a motion for reconsideration. I will not respond paragraph-by-paragraph to Defendant’s Statement of Material Facts, much of which recites background already before the Court or restates positions Defendant took prior to the June 11, 2026 hearing. I respond only where Defendant’s submission touches a specific, identifiable request for relief, or where the documentary record directly contradicts a factual assertion Defendant has made to this Court.

5.  The June 11, 2026 Order provides a workable structure for our co-parenting relationship. It allocates final decision-making authority over [Child]’s health, safety, welfare, and education to me; it establishes a defined regular and summer parenting-time schedule; it supplies a transportation and exchange provision; it fixes child-support and expense-sharing obligations; and it requires appropriate information-sharing through OFW. The problem is not that the Order is ambiguous or unworkable. The problem is that despite the Court’s June 2026 reaffirmation and clarification of the authority allocated to me in the December 2024 order—an issue previously raised—Defendant continues to act as though my final decision-making authority does not exist, treating each exercise of that authority without his agreement as grounds for renewed litigation.

6.  The volume of correspondence during the eight (8) days between entry of the June 11, 2026 Order and the date Defendant signed his motion (June 19, 2026) does not demonstrate that the Order is unworkable. It demonstrates Defendant’s refusal to allow the Order to operate. Within days of its entry, Defendant reopened issues the Court had just decided, imposed unilateral deadlines, challenged my exercise of the authority the Court granted me, conditioned payment of his existing obligations on reconsideration of separate financial issues, and repeatedly invoked further litigation. Reconsideration should not be granted merely because Defendant immediately generated disputes concerning provisions with which he disagrees.

7.  Moreover, we appeared in Court on the record on June 11, 2026, where the specific details of the parenting-time schedule and the pickup-and-drop-off structure were discussed at length before the Court entered its Order. Defendant was present for, and participated in, that discussion. His suggestion that he does not understand the language or intent of provisions he heard explained on the record, in his presence, only weeks earlier is not credible. His professed confusion reflects willful ignorance and a refusal to accept the authority the Court allocated to the Plaintiff, not a legitimate ambiguity requiring reconsideration or clarification.

***Custody Agreement Negotiations and Counsel Fees***

44. Defendant’s characterization of the parties’ prior custody agreement negotiations is incomplete and misleading. The draft agreement remained unexecuted because material terms remained unresolved, not because of any refusal on my part to engage in good-faith discussion. This issue was addressed on the record during one of our appearances before the court, where it was made clear that I did not agree—and do not agree—to execute the draft agreement in its prior form. Defendant cannot convert prior good-faith negotiations into a binding agreement or use an unsigned proposal as a basis for reconsideration or enforcement. His renewed attempt to impose selected provisions from that draft, including travel procedures and allocation of the dependency tax exemption, is therefore an attempt to impose terms that were never adopted by either party or the Court.

45. Defendant’s request for reimbursement of counsel fees rests on his unsupported characterization of my conduct as lacking good faith. I strongly reject that characterization. The eight (8) day chronology set forth above shows that I communicated with the Defendant on the issues he raised, attempted to address his questions, complied with the existing Orders, and accommodated his logistical requests without seeking immediate judicial intervention. Defendant appeared *pro se* at our most recent court appearance, yet he now seeks reimbursement for counsel fees incurred during the 2024 negotiations, unrelated to the court proceedings.

46. At the same time, Defendant has repeatedly invoked or threatened further litigation while knowing that I am represented and that continued motion practice imposes a substantial financial burden on me. He filed this motion only eight (8) days after the June 11 hearing and then expanded the issues through multiple supplemental submissions. Defendant should not be permitted to create or prolong litigation and then seek to impose the resulting counsel fees as an additional financial consequence to me. For these reasons, I renew my request that Defendant’s fee application be denied and that he reimburses me for the reasonable counsel fees and costs I incurred in opposing this motion and the Supplemental Submissions, in an amount to be certified by Counsel.

***Parenting Time Exchanges***

47. The June 11, 2026 Order establishes that the Sunday or Monday parenting-time exchange shall occur at New Jersey Turnpike Exit 8A at 5:30 p.m., when that exchange applies under the applicable schedule. (See **Exhibit A**; Transcript, pp. 44-45.) That provision was never intended to govern weekday daycare pickups. The Order separately and clearly provides that Defendant is to pick [Child] up from daycare for his parenting time. When Defendant asserted on June 28, 2026 that the Order did not require him to pick [Child] up from daycare and that I should pursue a modification if I wanted that arrangement, he was not identifying a genuine ambiguity in the Order; he was advancing an interpretation inconsistent with its structure and the exchange provisions discussed on the record. (See **Exhibit B**, p. 92.) Defendant should not be permitted to convert his disagreement with the weekday pickup arrangement into a request to modify a procedure the Court already established.

***Right of First Refusal***

48. The June 11, 2026 Order establishes a defined regular and summer parenting-time schedule. I object to imposing a right of first refusal in addition to that schedule. Defendant’s proposed right of first refusal is not a narrowly tailored provision addressing a demonstrated childcare need. Instead, it would require the parties to notify, consult, and coordinate with one another whenever either parent was unavailable for any period of time, however brief, including ordinary work obligations, daycare, family assistance, or other routine childcare. It would effectively subject every minor scheduling issue to negotiation and create precisely the type of recurring, granular disputes already reflected in the parties’ OFW communications. Given the parties’ communication history and the Defendant’s repeated use of routine scheduling issues to generate conflict and litigation, imposing such a provision would increase and not reduce discord and would not serve [Child]’s best interests. I therefore object to adding a right of first refusal to the clear parenting-time schedule already established by the June 11, 2026 Order.

***Support Calculation and Financial Discovery***

49. Defendant’s request for broad financial discovery into my income is invasive, overbroad, and unsupported by any competent evidence. Defendant identifies no evidence or specific basis to believe that income information used to calculate support was inaccurate; his request rests solely on speculation. I am submitting, together with this Certification, my 2025 income information. (See Plaintiff’s 2025 income information attached as **Exhibit F**). To the extent the Court wishes to review that information in connection with Defendant’s request, I do not object to that limited review. I object, however, to any broader financial discovery that is untethered to specific evidence or an actual error in the June 11, 2026 Order. A motion for reconsideration should not be used as a vehicle for a speculative fishing expedition into my finances or as another means for Defendant to increase my financial burden.

***Remaining Parenting Agreement Terms***

50. As set forth in my prior Certification filed with the Court, Defendant and I previously discussed a draft comprehensive custody and parenting time agreement that was never finalized or executed by either party. Defendant’s renewed attempt to enforce specific terms from that draft, including travel procedures and allocation of the dependency tax exemption, is not a request to reconsider any ruling the Court made. It is an attempt to unilaterally impose terms that never received my agreement and that were never adopted by the Court.

51. Defendant’s insistence that provisions from an unsigned proposal are somehow binding, or that this Court should compel their implementation now, reflects the same willful ignorance addressed throughout this Certification: Defendant treats my willingness to engage in good faith discussions as though it constituted agreement or a court order. I did not agree to those terms when the draft was under discussion, and I do not agree to them now. If Defendant wishes to propose terms for a future agreement, I remain willing to consider them directly or through counsel. However, the Court should not treat a stale, unexecuted draft as a basis for reconsideration, enforcement, or modification.

***Domestic Violence Evidence and Custody Considerations***

52. Defendant’s attempt to revive allegations of domestic violence and to portray me as physically abusive is misleading, overbroad, and irrelevant to the issues presently before the Court. To the extent Defendant relies on an isolated text message from a period when I was several months pregnant, that message does not establish the pattern of abuse or current parental danger he now suggests. The message also does not provide the full context of the parties’ relationship during that period, when I experienced substantial emotional abuse from Defendant. I do not ask the Court to adjudicate those competing historical allegations in this motion. Defendant identifies no new incident or changes in circumstances since entry of the existing Orders. His attempt to use previously known allegations to reopen custody, jurisdiction, or venue is improper, and I respectfully request that the Court reject it.

***Communication Schedule Certification***

53. Defendant’s second Supplemental Certification improperly attempts to inject a new dispute concerning the parties’ former informal FaceTime arrangement. His request for a fixed, multiple-times-per-week FaceTime schedule was not included in Defendant’s original Cross Application, his April 23, 2026 Certification, his Notice of Motion for Reconsideration, or the Reconciliation Table he submitted alongside this Certification. It arose only after I declined to continue an informal arrangement that Defendant is now attempting to impose on me as though it were a court-ordered obligation. Defendant is effectively using this motion to challenge my refusal to allow him to unilaterally convert that informal accommodation into a mandatory schedule.

54. The request is therefore not properly before the Court on a motion for reconsideration. In any event, as set forth above, the FaceTime arrangement was an informal accommodation—not a term of any Court Order—and was never subject to reconsideration or enforcement. Defendant cannot use this motion to obtain new relief concerning an arrangement that was neither ordered by the Court nor included in the issues he originally presented.

55. For these reasons, Defendant’s Reconciliation Table, Short Supplemental Legal Brief, and Supplemental Certifications fail to identify any newly discovered evidence, any controlling law overlooked by the Court, or any ruling decided on a palpably incorrect basis. Instead, Defendant’s supplemental submissions attempt to expand the scope of his motion by rearguing matters already addressed, introducing issues that were not raised in his original filings, and seeking to impose obligations based on informal arrangements, speculation, or unexecuted proposals. Nothing in those submissions provides a proper basis for reconsideration. As explained above, Defendant’s Motion for Reconsideration and all Supplemental Submissions should therefore be denied in their entirety.

**<u>REQUESTS FOR COUNSEL FEES</u>**

56. Defendant requests reconsideration of the Court’s fee determination while also requesting an award of his own attorney’s fees and litigation expenses. Defendant is self-represented in connection with this application and identifies no attorney’s fees currently being incurred in prosecuting this motion. To the extent he relies on billing records from prior counsel, those records concern earlier proceedings and that representation concluded before this application was filed, and do not establish fees incurred in connection with this motion. Defendant has not identified any ruling warranting reconsideration, any bad faith conduct by me, or any other basis for shifting his prior litigation expenses to me.

57. The timing and substance of his request demonstrate a disregard for the Court’s resources and for the financial burden imposed on me by forcing a new round of litigation almost immediately after the June 11 hearing. Defendant’s request is particularly unwarranted because, rather accept the Court’s June 11 ruling, he filed this motion within eight (8) days and continued expanding the issues presented. He has pursued collateral matters while fully aware that I am represented and must incur counsel fees to respond. His submissions have required my counsel to review an extensive and continually expanding record, including multiple certifications, supplemental submissions, exhibits, financial issues, expense records, and new requests that were not included in the original motion. This unnecessary use of judicial and party resources is precisely the type of conduct for which Defendant now seeks to shift fees to me. Because Defendant has not identified any ruling warranting reconsideration, and because his unsupported and prematurely filed application has forced me to incur substantial fees and costs, I respectfully request that the Court require Defendant to reimburse me for the reasonable counsel fees and costs incurred in opposing this motion, in an amount to be certified by my counsel.

**<u>SUPPORT FOR AFFIRMATIVE REQUESTS FOR RELIEF</u>**

58. I am not merely opposing Defendant’s motion. As detailed throughout this Certification, Defendant has refused to acknowledge my court-allocated decision-making authority, treated informal arrangements and unexecuted proposals as though they were binding orders, conditioned his compliance with existing financial obligations on relief he has not obtained, and the repeatedly expanded disputes that were already addressed or were never before the Court. He filed his application only eight days after the June 11, 2026 hearing, thereby imposing another unnecessary burden on both the Court and me. In light of that conduct, I affirmatively cross-request the relief set forth below and in the Conclusion. I address the factual basis for each request in turn.

**<u>Enforcement of Decision-Making Authority</u>**

59. As detailed above, including in connection with the Chinese-school, financial decisions, and recent-communications sections, Defendant continues to act as though the decision-making authority allocated to me by the December 6, 2024 and June 11, 2026 Orders is subject to his approval. I respectfully request that the Court expressly reaffirm that I have final decision-making authority over the matters assigned to me under those Orders. The Court should further clarify that Defendant’s right to receive appropriate information and records does not give him joint final authority, a veto, or the right to condition my exercise of that authority on his agreement. Without the clarification, I expect Defendant is likely to continue treating each decision within my authority as a basis for dispute and renewed motion practice.

**<u>Appointment of a Parenting Coordinator</u>**

60. The volume, frequency, and character of the disputes reflected in the Our Family Wizard logs attached to this Certification, and in Defendant’s own Exhibit AG, demonstrate that the parties require assistance implementing the June 11, 2026 Order without repeatedly returning to Court. A Parenting Coordinator would allow routine implementation disputes, such as those concerning Chinese school, medical-appointment notice, exchange logistics, extracurricular activities, and school-related matters to be addressed promptly and without escalating into further litigation I respectfully request that the Court appoint [Proposed Parenting Coordinator], Esq. as our Parenting Coordinator, subject to her availability and any required conflict review, with authority limited to the implementation disputes rather than the modification of the Court’s Orders or either party’s decision-making authority. I am willing to share the cost of a parenting coordinator equally with Defendant, although the record reflects that his conduct has generated the substantial majority of the disputes requiring intervention.

**<u>Restriction on the Subject Matter of Defendant’s Communication</u>**

61. Defendant’s OFW messages routinely extend beyond necessary information about [Child] and his parenting time schedule and instead devolve into accusations, unilateral deadlines, repeated demands, and threats of further litigation. Based on the communication history described above, I respectfully request a mutual, prospective communication protocol requiring concise, topic-specific OFW messages concerning [Child]’s schedule, exchanges, health and safety, school or daycare, extracurricular activities, and other matters directly affecting him. The protocol should require reasonable communication hours except for genuine emergencies, prohibit repetitive follow-up messages and unilateral deadlines, and prevent either party from using OFW to relitigate issues already addressed by the Court. It should preserve both parties’ right to receive information and seek appropriate judicial relief.

**<u>Enforcement of Child Support and Expense-Sharing Obligations</u>**

62. As detailed above and reflected in the updated expense records, Defendant has repeatedly delayed, disputed, selectively paid, or refused shared expenses while continuing to insist that I satisfy obligations he claims are owed to him. I respectfully request the Court direct Defendant to pay all properly documented past-due expense balances identified in the updated OFW within seven days, after crediting any appropriate payments. I further request that the Court establish a payment schedule requiring Defendant to pay \$100 per week toward the \$3,801.56 in child support arrears reflected by the Probation Division, in addition to his ongoing support obligation, until those arrears are paid in full. Any payment schedule should be separate from, and should not be used to delay, Defendant’s ongoing expense sharing obligations.

**<u>Passport Documentation</u>**

63. [Child] does not currently have a passport. Because the parties share legal custody, his passport application requires Defendant either to appear in person with [Child] or, if he does not appear, to provide his notarized consent using Form DS-3053. The parties previously discussed this issue through OFW. I advised Defendant that no appointment was available on June 19 because it was Juneteenth, a federal holiday, and asked him to provide his availability after June 29 so that I could arrange an appointment. (See **Exhibit C**, pp. 10 and 27.) Defendant responded that he would be unavailable for the remainder of June and would provide alternative dates. (See **Exhibit B**, p. 55.) He has not provided any dates, attended an appointment, or executed the required consent, and [Child]’s passport application therefore remains incomplete.

64. Requiring the parties to coordinate and attend an appointment together is unnecessary because the Department of State permits a non-appearing parent who shares custody to provide notarized consent using Form DS-3053. I therefore respectfully request that the Court direct Defendant, within seven (7) days, to: (a) complete, sign, and have notarized the current Form DS-3053; (b) return the original notarized form to me; (c) provide a legible photocopy of the front and back of the government-issued photographic identification he presented to the notary; and (d) provide any additional information or documentation reasonably required by the passport acceptance facility. I will arrange the passport appointment promptly so that the consent form can be submitted within the applicable ninety-day period. The current Form DS-3053 requiring Defendant’s signature is attached as **Exhibit G**.

Confirm that Defendant’s right to appropriate information does not create joint final authority or a veto over those decisions allocated to me.

    5.  Confirm that [Child]’s enrollment in Chinese school falls within my court-allocated educational decision-making authority and that his enrollment does not constitute interference with Defendant’s parenting time. Direct the parties to make reasonable, good-faith efforts to facilitate [Child]’s attendance, taking into account his age, the travel distance from Defendant’s residence, and [Child]’s comfort and best interests. I am not requesting that [Child] be subjected to excessive travel solely to attend a class during Defendant’s parenting time.

    6.  Clarify that the Sunday or Monday return exchange at New Jersey Turnpike Exit 8A applies only to the return exchange identified in the June 11, 2026 Order and does not govern Defendant’s weekday pickup of [Child] from daycare at the beginning of his parenting time. Direct both parties to follow the ordered exchange procedures unless they agree to a deviation in writing through OFW, and require reasonable advance notice of any requested nonemergency change.

    7.  Deny Defendant’s request for a fixed, multiple-times-per-week FaceTime schedule. Confirm that I may facilitate reasonable, child-focused video communication between [Child] and Defendant when appropriate or when requested by [Child], without being required to reinstate or maintain the parties’ former informal schedule.

    8.  Deny Defendant’s request for a right of first refusal as overbroad, unnecessary, likely to create additional conflict, and inconsistent with the defined parenting-time schedule established by the June 11, 2026 Order.

    9.  Appoint [Proposed Parenting Coordinator], Esq., subject to her availability and completion of any required conflict review, as Parenting Coordinator. Limit her authority to facilitating implementation of the existing Orders and resolving routine parenting-time and logistical disputes, without authority to modify the Orders or override either party’s court-allocated decision-making authority. Direct that the cost be shared equally by the parties.

    10. Establish a reciprocal OFW communication protocol requiring the parties to use concise, topic-specific messages limited to matters directly affecting [Child], including his schedule, exchanges, health and safety, school or daycare, extracurricular activities, and information required under the Orders. Except in emergencies, require nonurgent communications to be sent during reasonable hours, prohibit repetitive follow-up messages before the other party has had a reasonable opportunity to respond, and prohibit the use of OFW to repeatedly reargue issues already resolved by the Court. Preserve both parties’ ability to communicate regarding genuine emergencies and to obtain information to which they are entitled.

    11. Deny Defendant’s request that I undergo a mental health evaluation, counseling, or treatment as unsupported by competent evidence, unrelated to any demonstrated impairment of my parenting ability or risk to [Child], and outside any proper basis for reconsideration on the present record, expressly and with prejudice.

    12. Enforce the June 11, 2026 Order’s provisions concerning ongoing child support and the allocation and timely reimbursement of daycare, medical, educational, and extracurricular expenses.

    17. Direct Defendant, within seven days, to execute and return all documents requiring his signature that are necessary to obtain a passport for [Child] and to cooperate promptly with any required appointment or alternative consent procedure.

    18. Order Defendant to reimburse Plaintiff for the reasonable counsel fees and costs incurred in opposing this motion and the Supplemental Submissions, in an amount to be certified by counsel (see Certification of Services attached as **Exhibit H**).

    19. Grant such other and further relief as the Court deems equitable and just.
