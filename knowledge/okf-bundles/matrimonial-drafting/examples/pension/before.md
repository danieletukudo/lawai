# Pension-credit certification (earlier draft)

Anonymized reference sample. Dates and financial figures are fictional. Names and identifying details are generalized. Full narrative retained; referenced exhibits are excluded.

**Plaintiff v Defendant**

Client is Plaintiff. This is a working document, not a confirmed filed version.

I, Plaintiff, certify as follows:

1\. I am the Plaintiff in this matter. Defendant is my former wife. I submit this certification to obtain an accurate calculation of the retirement credits required by our Marital Settlement Agreement (MSA). I ask that the accounting recognize payments made through deductions from my pension for Defendant’s benefit, so that I am not required to satisfy the same pension obligation twice.

2\. Except where I identify information from my attorney or the accountant’s report, these facts are based on my personal knowledge and pension and bank records. I ask the jointly retained accountant to verify the payments, calculate their tax treatment, and incorporate the credit.

3\. We married on November 25, 2021. The Complaint for Divorce was filed on September 30, 2048. My Employer Pension Plan A (Plan A) pension was already in pay status when the Complaint was filed.

4\. The Court entered the Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO) dividing my Plan A pension on May 30, 2052. A copy is attached as Exhibit B. Paragraph 5 directs immediate withholding of Defendant’s marital share; paragraph 11 requires return to the Plan Administrator of her benefits mistakenly paid to me. The Pension Administrator (Administrator) implemented regular withholding for August 2052. Defendant’s Employer Plan B and Employer Plan C pensions were also in pay status by May 30, but only my pension was then divided. Reciprocal credits and division or offset of the other retirement assets remained to be calculated.

5\. The accountant’s draft states that Defendant began receiving her Employer Plan B pension in February 2052 and her Employer Plan C pension in April 2052. It calculates credits due to me for my marital share of those payments. I seek the same complete accounting for payments concerning my pension, including amounts already collected from me for Defendant’s share.

6\. Paragraph 5 of the May 8, 2053 Consent Order requires calculation of marital retirement interests and tax-effected credits for payments received before division, followed by equal division plus or minus credits. That order is the MSA’s internal Exhibit B. The executed MSA with its attachments is attached as Exhibit A to this motion.

7\. We executed the MSA on May 20, 2054. Paragraph 38 preserves the Consent Order’s retirement provisions. Paragraph 40 records the January 16, 2054 order requiring the Accountant to calculate retirement interests and tax-effected credits. Paragraph 42 requires equal division of the marital portions “plus or minus any credits.” Exhibit A.

8\. When I entered the MSA, I understood that the accountant would calculate the credits due to each of us before the retirement division was completed. I did not agree that pension amounts collected from me for Defendant’s benefit could be disregarded, or that she could receive an additional payment for an obligation already satisfied through the pension administrator.

9\. Following the QDRO, my pension deposits decreased when Defendant’s regular share was separated from my payment. The Bank A extracts enclosed with my attorney’s August 19, 2054 letter show a pension deposit of \$16,997.86 on August 1, 2052. The letter identifies subsequent deposits of approximately \$9,850.42 after the regular division. Those records are attached as Exhibit D.

10\. The Administrator’s August 22, 2052 notice identified a separate overpayment and a \$678.00 monthly repayment beginning November 1, 2052. The notice, reproduced on page 2 of the email chain, is attached as Exhibit C. It separately sets my regular allowance at \$11,501.24 beginning with the September 1 check for August. That allowance differs from my net bank deposits. The additional repayment was separate from the regular division of ongoing benefits.

11\. The Administrator identified an overpayment of \$16,244.20 and stated that Defendant was entitled to a portion of my benefits effective June 2052, for which it had no record of her receipt. The letter gave me an opportunity to verify any personal payments for equitable distribution and stated that, absent verification, the Administrator would collect \$678.00 monthly beginning November 1, 2052 until the overpayment was satisfied. The total equals two shares of \$8,122.10, the amount used in the Accountant’s draft for June and July 2052. I ask the Accountant to reconcile those same benefits with the Administrator’s collections.

12\. I received reduced pension deposits after the repayment schedule began. The enclosed bank extract reflects a pension deposit of \$9,253.78 on November 29. I understand that the additional deductions were being used to recover the pension benefits assigned to Defendant that had previously been included in my payment. Those deductions reduced the pension income available to me.

13\. The bank records show net deposits rather than a complete gross repayment history. the Accountant should use the Administrator’s notice, pension statements, and repayment ledger to verify the deductions and tax adjustments, rather than calculate the credit from net deposit differences alone.

14\. I had previously supplied the Administrator’s letter to my attorney before trial preparation. On July 29, 2054 I emailed my attorney under the subject “Additional Retrospective Pension Deductions for June & July 2052,” forwarding the Administrator’s notice. I reminded counsel of these deductions because I wanted to ensure that the accountant credited payments already being made from my pension. My email is included in the chain attached as Exhibit F.

15\. My attorney has advised me that she received the Accountant’s initial draft on July 7, 2054. The report is marked as a draft for discussion and preliminary purposes. It states that its conclusions are based on information received to date and may change if additional information or documentation is received. A copy is attached as Exhibit E.

16\. The draft charges me with Defendant’s share of my pension at \$8,122.10 per month for 46 months, from October 2048 through July 2052. It calculates \$373,616.60 before tax and \$317,354.82 after tax. June and July 2052 are included in that calculation. The draft does not separately identify or credit the Administrator’s \$16,244.20 recoupment.

17\. I do not dispute that Defendant is entitled to her proper share of the marital pension benefits. My objection is to charging me for the full June and July amounts in the accountant’s netting while those same amounts have been or are being collected from my share under the Administrator’s repayment schedule. The obligation for a given month must take account of payments already made toward that obligation.

18\. On July 31, 2054 my attorney forwarded my email and the Administrator’s notice to the Accountant. Counsel wrote, “I know we are still waiting for Defendant to approve the draft report,” and asked whether the matter had been considered in the analysis. This email is part of Exhibit F. The request was made 24 days after counsel received the initial draft, while my attorney understood that Defendant’s approval remained pending.

19\. On August 3, 2054 the Accountant replied that her analysis included payments owed to Defendant through July 2052. She identified the \$16,244.20 overpayment in the Administrator’s letter, asked whether the \$678.00 reduction had occurred, and stated that an adjustment might be needed if it had. On August 4, 2054 my attorney confirmed that the \$678.00 had been deducted and requested the adjustment. Both emails are in Exhibit F.

20\. On August 19, 2054, my attorney wrote to Defendant’s attorney, explaining the additional pension deductions and requesting written agreement to the credit within ten days. The letter enclosed Bank A extracts and was copied to the Accountant and me. A copy of the letter is attached as Exhibit G. Its bank enclosures are attached separately as Exhibit D.

21\. The August 19 correspondence supplied Defendant’s counsel with records of my reduced deposits and explained the separate recoupment. My attorney has advised me that the Administrator’s notice was also previously supplied to Defendant’s counsel. Defendant has not agreed to the credit.

22\. On August 31, 2054, Defendant emailed my attorney rejecting recalculation. Her email, attached as Exhibit H, states that the letter was produced after finalization, that my attorney agreed on July 10 that the report was satisfactory and should be finalized, and that recalculation would impose costs on her. She also stated that she would seek attorney’s fees if required to retain counsel. My attorney has advised me that the Accountant has not issued a final report as of the date of this certification. The July 31 email states that her approval was pending, and the Accountant considered a possible adjustment on August 3. I did not personally agree to give up the recoupment credit or sign a writing waiving it.

23\. I knew that my pension deposits had been reduced. What required attention during review of the draft was whether the additional repayment had been reflected in the accountant’s calculation of what remained owing between us. Knowing that money was being collected from me did not mean that I agreed to let the same obligation be included again in a separate settlement calculation.

24\. I understand that the MSA required documents to be supplied to the Accountant within ten days. I am not asking to disregard that requirement. I ask the Court to allow the accountant to reconcile an identified payment issue before her work becomes final. The existing pension account, our agreed shares, and the agreed method of division remain the same. I have not agreed to forfeit a payment credit because a document was brought to the accountant’s attention during review of her draft.

25\. The draft also calculates credits to me from Defendant’s Employer Plan B and Employer Plan C pension payments and an interest in her Bank B IRA. Its proposed net pre-tax transfer to me is \$179,602.80. Because my pension arrears are subtracted in calculating that transfer, omitting a payment credit affects the net distribution as well as the individual pension calculation. I ask the Accountant to revise both consistently, without counting any credit twice.

26\. Based on the Administrator’s recoupment and my reduced deposits, it is my understanding that money collected from my pension under this schedule is applied toward the June and July obligation for Defendant’s benefit. I do not have personal access to Defendant’s pension receipts or bank account. I ask that the Administrator’s allocation and disbursement records and Defendant’s corresponding pension records be provided to the Accountant to establish what was applied to her June and July entitlement. The notice and my net deposits alone do not establish every amount actually disbursed to her.

27\. I request credit for amounts verified as collected from me and applied to the obligation included in the draft. Any remaining scheduled portion of the \$16,244.20 should be addressed expressly so that it is paid once. I seek a procedure preventing duplicate payment or an unpaid balance, without treating future deductions as already collected.

28\. I ask the Accountant to identify the pension months, verify the collections and Defendant’s allocations and receipts, determine the outstanding balance and tax effect, and revise the pension arrears and net distribution. I will provide the pension and bank records needed for that reconciliation.

29\. I also request reasonable counsel fees and costs incurred to obtain enforcement, as permitted by paragraph 58 of the MSA and as supported by my attorney’s separate fee submission. I attempted to have this issue resolved through counsel before seeking Court intervention.

30\. I respectfully request enforcement of the MSA and a complete reconciliation by the Accountant. The final accounting should credit payments collected from my pension and establish what remains due, without a second recovery of the same benefits.

I certify that the foregoing statements made by me are true. I am aware that if any of the foregoing statements made by me are willfully false, I am subject to punishment.

Date \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

**Plaintiff**
