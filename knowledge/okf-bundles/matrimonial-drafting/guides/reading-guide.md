# Reading guide for the drafting examples

These two examples provide complete narrative documents for reviewing drafting quality and designing an AI workflow. Read each earlier draft beside its later revision. The pension example also includes the complete client comments.

**Files in reading order**

**01_Pension_Before.docx**

Earlier pension-credit certification with 30 numbered paragraphs.

**02_Pension_Client_Comments.docx**

Client feedback covering chronology, updated accounting, pending issues, and property distribution.

**03_Pension_After.docx**

Updated pension-credit certification with 33 numbered paragraphs.

**04_Support_Before.docx**

Client’s initial support certification with 25 numbered paragraphs and two subparagraphs.

**05_Support_After.docx**

Developed support certification with 45 numbered paragraphs.

**Anonymization and scope**

The parties are Plaintiff and Defendant throughout. The client is Plaintiff in Example One and Defendant in Example Two. Dates, years, adult ages, and financial figures are fictionalized consistently across each set. Financial relationships, duration calculations, paragraph references, and exhibit labels are retained. Employers, banks, locations, professions, retailers, and distinctive property descriptions are replaced with generic equivalents.

The narrative paragraphs, client comments, substantive headings, and certification language are retained in full, subject to anonymization. Original letterheads, court captions, case identifiers, contact details, signatures, document metadata, and blank exhibit dividers are excluded. The documents are rebuilt in a uniform Word format. Source exhibits are not included; exhibit references alone are not evidence available to an agent.

**How to interpret the revisions**

The pension revision distinguishes the client’s delivery of a record to counsel from counsel’s later transmission to the accountant, expands the requested accounting, and addresses outstanding implementation issues. The earlier certification already reflects substantial drafting; its authorship and status as an associate’s first draft are not established.

The support pair shows development of an initial client account into a fuller factual narrative and specific requested relief. The later document draws on additional case information beyond the initial draft. Do not infer that its new factual allegations can be generated from the initial draft alone.

The later drafts are selected reference work product, not confirmed filed versions or representations of court rulings. Original typos, disputed assertions, and differences between versions remain unless anonymization required a change. They require attorney review before use as legal templates or evaluation targets.

**Suggested evaluation**

Assess whether an agent preserves the client’s facts and voice, answers each material comment, distinguishes evidence from inference, maintains arithmetic and chronological consistency, connects requested relief to the factual record, and identifies missing inputs. Any added fact must be supported by an actual source; the later document should not be used to invent facts absent from the earlier input.

The ISC example is not included because the original draft, client response, and completed statement have not yet been located.
