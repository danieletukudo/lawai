# SOP 3: Letter briefs

## Purpose and required result

Prepare focused advocacy that tells the court what relief to grant, why the governing law permits it, and how the actual record supports it. The brief must develop the reasoning rather than merely summarize pleadings or collect case quotations. The letter form does not reduce the need for complete analysis.

## Inputs and readiness

Obtain the application and proposed order, supporting and opposing certifications, relevant agreements and orders, exhibits, court directions, authorized legal research, and the hearing or filing schedule. Confirm the side represented, precise question assigned by the court, permissible scope, page cap, research restrictions, and whether a reply raises a separate procedural issue.

Confirm the recipient, address, docket, judge, hearing date, and current counsel block. Do not copy filing dates or judge names from an old sample. Determine whether the court expects a letter brief, formal brief, supplemental submission, or another format.

## Preparation procedure

1\. Identify the dispositive issues and the precise outcome requested. Separate the threshold showing from the ultimate merits and identify which party bears each burden.

2\. Build an authority matrix matching each proposition to the actual authority, relevant passage, court level, factual context, and verification status. Read the agreement’s exact language before asserting waiver, nonmodifiability, or automatic termination.

3\. Organize point headings around the requested determination and its reason. The headings read together should show the court the proposed path to decision.

4\. Write a preliminary statement identifying the relief, decisive facts, and central legal basis. Include primary and alternative relief only where counsel has authorized them. Avoid an extended opening that repeats the entire factual record.

5\. Provide the procedural history and facts necessary to decide the issues. Distinguish allegations, documentary facts, admitted facts, and findings. Explain what prior orders decided and what they reserved.

6\. Develop each point using the analytical sequence below. Address the strongest opposing position and unfavorable facts directly, then explain their legal consequence or limitation.

7\. Connect requested sums to reliable calculations and available proof. Do not present a target bonus as received cash, gross assets as spendable liquidity, or a requested litigation fund as justified merely by case complexity.

8\. Draft a precise conclusion that can be translated into an order. Match amounts, timing, production obligations, and reservations to the application and factual certification.

9\. Verify authorities, quotations, pinpoints, evidence references, and current law within the permitted research scope. Avoid claiming that a case decided the present issue when the argument depends on analogy.

10\. Check the rendered Word document, applicable length, headings, letter elements, and signature block. Provide a separate internal gap list instead of placing editorial instructions in the court-facing argument.

## Required analytical sequence for each point

| **Element**             | **What the paragraph must accomplish**                                                                               |
|-------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Requested determination | State the decision sought and the issue it resolves.                                                                 |
| Rule and burden         | Explain the applicable standard, threshold, and burden using verified authority.                                     |
| Relevant authority      | State what the case or text actually establishes and why its context matters.                                        |
| Application             | Connect specific record facts to the elements or factors. Explain the inference rather than leaving it to the judge. |
| Counterargument         | Address the material adverse authority, fact, or contractual language and explain the response.                      |
| Remedy                  | State the resulting order, necessary findings, limits, and any authorized alternative.                               |

## Drafting example

Weak: “The marital lifestyle was substantial. Therefore, the court should award the requested support.”

Acceptable approach: Identify the documented lifestyle, present needs and resources, current ability to pay, the interim standard, and the calculation supporting the request. Explain how a direct bill-payment obligation interacts with a cash award so expenses are not counted twice. Where proof is incomplete, identify a supported interim amount or another alternative authorized by counsel, with a defined reservation for further review.

When applying a final-award case to an interim request, state that the case addressed the final award and explain the proposed analogy. When enforcing an MSA, distinguish the operative words from what a party argues those words mean. The force of the argument should come from those connections.

## Acceptance checklist and handoff

- Every material requested determination has a rule, record application, and resulting remedy.

- The brief answers the assigned court question and addresses material opposing arguments without expanding beyond the authorized submission.

- Quotations, holdings, citations, and pinpoints are accurate; contrary facts and limiting context have not been hidden.

- The argument relies on facts in the proper record and is consistent with the certification and proposed order.

- Point headings, body spacing, page numbers, and letter format follow the matter instructions.

- Deliver the Word brief and a concise attorney note listing unresolved proof, authority, or relief questions. Generate a table of authorities only when required or requested.

Reject the draft if it contains a legal conclusion without application, asserts a factual finding never made, or uses a legal citation only as decoration. A strong opening cannot compensate for missing analysis in the individual points.
