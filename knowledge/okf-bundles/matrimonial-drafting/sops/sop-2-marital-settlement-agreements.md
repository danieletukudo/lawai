# SOP 2: Marital settlement agreements

## Purpose and required result

Prepare a comprehensive marital settlement agreement (MSA) that accurately memorializes the approved bargain and tells the parties how to perform it. The agreement must distinguish agreed terms, open terms, reservations, and implementation details requiring approval. Comprehensive drafting does not authorize new economic or parenting concessions.

## Inputs and readiness

Obtain the current term sheet or settlement notes, latest MSA and redline, attorney and client directions, both parties’ available Case Information Statements (CISs), controlling orders, civil restraints, property and debt information, support calculations, and relevant specialist or institutional instructions. Identify the actual accepted baseline and the status of every proposed change. A mediation recommendation or adversary proposal is not automatically an agreed term.

Confirm children and their identifying details as appropriate, assets and liabilities, alimony position, support mechanism, effective dates, mediator or negotiation history, counsel identities, and any prior agreement that survives separately. If an input is missing, draft unaffected sections and preserve the gap in the review log. A blank awaiting the other party’s CIS is not permission to invent that party’s holdings.

## Preparation procedure

1\. Build a term reconciliation table. For each issue, record the controlling source, exact approved term, approval status, draft paragraph, open question, and any material departure. Reconcile emails, meeting notes, comments, and redlines before drafting.

2\. Start from an appropriate firm precedent. Remove unrelated facts and inapplicable clauses. Preserve required protections and the specific agreement’s structure where the assignment is only a targeted edit.

3\. Draft the recitals accurately, including the parties, marriage, children if applicable, proceeding, counsel, and negotiation history. Describe disclosure and independent advice only to the extent they actually occurred.

4\. Draft each operative obligation using the performance checklist below. Resolve inconsistent triggers, percentages, payment periods, and exceptions. If an implementation term affects rights, cost, timing, discretion, or access to court, obtain approval rather than treating it as clerical.

5\. Address the relevant parenting, support, property, debt, tax, insurance, fee, and general provisions. Use the coverage matrix to identify omissions; mark an issue inapplicable or reserved rather than importing an unnecessary obligation.

6\. Reconcile interconnected provisions. Expense allocation must match support calculations and reimbursement terms. Asset transfers must match releases and indemnities. General modification language must not unintentionally undo a specific negotiated waiver or reservation.

7\. Review schedules and attachments against the operative text. Ensure the agreement accurately identifies external documents that are incorporated, referenced, superseded, or preserved. Where civil restraints survive without merger, say so as directed and avoid contradictory boilerplate.

8\. For a returned redline, classify each change as editorial, implementing an approved term, substantive, or unresolved. Compare the operative text rather than relying on counsel’s comment that a request was accepted. Never accept all changes globally merely to obtain a clean copy.

9\. Recalculate balances, buyouts, credits, percentages, and payment totals. Check for duplicate distributions, mismatched valuation dates, and crediting the same payment twice. Confirm tax or plan mechanics with the appropriate professional when necessary.

10\. Prepare the requested clean draft and genuine redline, preserve requested comments, and provide a short decision list. After approval, remove unresolved placeholders and rejected alternatives from the execution version, while retaining the negotiation history internally.

## Performance checklist for every obligation

| **Field**                 | **Drafting question**                                                                      |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Responsible person        | Who must act, pay, sign, disclose, transport, or cooperate?                                |
| Required performance      | What exact action or payment is required, and to whom?                                     |
| Amount or formula         | Is the amount, percentage, valuation method, or calculation complete and unambiguous?      |
| Trigger and timing        | When does performance start, occur, repeat, and end?                                       |
| Mechanism and proof       | How is it performed, documented, and confirmed?                                            |
| Conditions and exceptions | What consent, notice, approval, eligibility, or event changes the obligation?              |
| Failure and remedy        | What agreed process applies if performance fails, becomes impossible, or remains disputed? |

Do not silently add deadlines, consent thresholds, reimbursement periods, releases, or penalties. An omitted implementation detail should generate a proposed term for attorney approval when it changes the bargain.

## Coverage and consistency matrix

| **Area**                   | **Questions to resolve when applicable**                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Custody and parenting      | Legal and physical arrangements, school designation, regular schedule, holidays, vacations, exchanges, school closures, activities, travel documents, communications, and any temporary without-prejudice treatment.                                           |
| Child support and expenses | Payment or joint-account mechanism, contribution timing, covered costs, consent thresholds, reimbursement proof, account administration, review, and avoidance of duplicate charges. Court review and child-related rights require appropriate legal analysis. |
| Alimony                    | Amount or mutual waiver, duration and triggers, agreed modification treatment, lifestyle provisions, security, and relationship to the property bargain. Use counsel-approved language and verify applicable law.                                              |
| Real estate                | Sale or buyout, valuation, broker selection, expenses, possession, credits, proceeds, escrow, cooperation, and unresolved offsets.                                                                                                                             |
| Accounts and retirement    | Specific holdings or approved general waivers, allocation dates, gains and losses, transfer mechanism, fees, and tax consequences. A qualified domestic relations order is not the automatic mechanism for every account.                                      |
| Business and debt          | Interests retained or distributed, guarantees, repayment, restrictions, access to records, releases, indemnification, and practical limits on binding creditors or nonparties.                                                                                 |
| Insurance and tax          | Coverage, premium responsibility, beneficiary treatment, termination events, tax filings, refunds and liabilities, available benefits, and required cooperation. Verify current law.                                                                           |
| Other terms                | Personal property, fees, dispute resolution, emergency relief, prior orders, survival and merger, disclosure, execution, further assurances, and reserved issues.                                                                                              |

## Drafting example

Weak: “The parties shall equally share all children’s expenses.”

Acceptable approach: Identify which expenses are shared, whether ordinary support already includes them, any agreed advance-consent threshold, the percentage, the submission and payment process, documentation, and disputes. If the approved term is only “50/50,” identify the missing implementation decisions for counsel rather than inventing them.

An agreement that lets each party retain individual assets should not represent that no individual assets exist. A clause deferring a future decision should not leave another sentence imposing that same obligation now. These contradictions require substantive reconciliation even when the assignment begins as proofreading.

## Acceptance checklist and handoff

- Every approved term appears accurately, every material departure is disclosed, and every open term is visibly reserved for review.

- Each obligation can be performed without the reader guessing who acts, when, or how much is owed.

- Schedules, percentages, support calculations, asset transfers, credits, and cross-references reconcile.

- Specific negotiated provisions are consistent with modification, merger, release, integration, and enforcement boilerplate.

- No signature-ready draft contains an unresolved economic concession, incomplete schedule, or factual representation unsupported by the disclosures.

- Deliver the requested Word version, tracked redline when requested, and an issue list identifying the decision, source, consequence, and approval needed.

Reject the draft if it silently changes the bargain, invents disclosure or consent, misstates a creditor release, or substitutes familiar boilerplate for the parties’ actual terms. A property waiver, support arrangement, and custody provision each require the relevant legal review; contract wording alone does not establish enforceability.
