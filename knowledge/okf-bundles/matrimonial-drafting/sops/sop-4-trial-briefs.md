# SOP 4: Trial briefs

## Purpose and required result

Prepare the court’s issue-by-issue roadmap for deciding the case. For each disputed issue, identify the governing framework, evidence, opposing position, and exact proposed determination. The brief should show both the legal basis and the practical result of the requested judgment.

## Inputs and readiness

Obtain the pretrial order and judge’s directions, operative pleadings, relevant interim and final orders, stipulations, current and historic financial disclosures where relevant, expert reports, valuation and tracing records, proposed exhibits, witness information, trial transcripts if testimony has occurred, and counsel’s requested outcome on each issue.

Determine whether this is a pretrial brief, midtrial submission, or post-trial summation. Before trial, describe anticipated proof accurately. After testimony, use the actual record and identify what was admitted, excluded, contradicted, or never offered. Confirm counsel and co-counsel credit, the issues reserved for trial, deadlines, page cap, and exhibit numbering status.

## Preparation procedure

1\. Create a complete issue inventory from pleadings, pretrial directions, stipulations, orders, and current instructions. Separate settled matters from remaining disputes. Identify relief requiring a threshold showing, specific notice, joinder, or another procedural prerequisite for attorney review.

2\. Develop a concise theory explaining the requested outcome across the case. Test it against adverse evidence and inconsistent positions. Avoid describing the client as entitled to a result merely because the overall narrative is sympathetic.

3\. Build a procedural chronology from actual entered documents. Distinguish requested relief from granted relief, and preserve reservations. A handwritten denial on a proposed order controls over the unchecked requested restrictions printed in the form.

4\. Build a separate proof table for every issue. Identify each supporting and contrary exhibit, witness, transcript reference if applicable, burden, gap, and requested finding. Use provisional internal identifiers if formal trial marks have not been assigned.

5\. Draft the applicable statutory and doctrinal framework, then analyze each disputed issue at the necessary level of detail. For financial distribution, address each material asset or coherent asset class specifically, including classification, tracing, valuation, debt, credits, and transfer mechanics.

6\. Apply facts and evidence to every relevant element or factor. Address both parties’ relevant CISs and expert evidence where instructed; do not cite only the favorable version. Use dates and financial cutoffs supported by the record and governing law, rather than treating a prior matter’s cutoff as universal.

7\. Separate principal theories from alternatives. Explain the legal and evidentiary basis for each and avoid duplicate recovery. For a remedy affecting a business, professional practice, lender, or other nonparty, identify authority, jurisdiction, notice, feasibility, and implementation issues for counsel rather than assuming the court can bind everyone named.

8\. State proposed findings and operative relief for each issue. Describe sequencing, offsets, deadlines if authorized, allocation of costs, required documentation, and reserved matters. Check that the requested judgment could actually be performed.

9\. Reconcile the brief with the exhibit list, witness plan, proposed judgment, calculations, and requested relief. If an exhibit is missing, identify what proposition lacks proof; do not list it as received or admitted.

10\. Verify citations and record references, complete counsel credit, render the final brief, then generate the table of authorities from that version. Recheck page references whenever the brief changes.

## Recommended organization

| **Component**         | **Required content**                                                                                                               |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Cover                 | Accurate caption, document title, proceeding, judge and date if required, and all authorized attorney and co-counsel designations. |
| Preliminary statement | The case theory, principal disputes, and proposed resolution.                                                                      |
| Procedural history    | Relevant filings, entered orders, stipulations, and issues expressly reserved. A compact orders chart may help.                    |
| Facts and evidence    | Supported facts organized for the disputed issues, with appropriate references and clear status.                                   |
| Legal points          | Governing framework, issue-specific application, contrary position, proof, and requested determination.                            |
| Conclusion            | A complete, consistent list or narrative of the relief sought, including authorized alternatives and reservations.                 |
| Supporting tables     | Contents, authorities, asset calculations, or other tools required by the court or useful for this assignment.                     |

## Required depth for financial and parenting issues

For a property issue, identify the actual asset, relevant ownership and transaction history, competing classifications, valuation evidence and date, liabilities, claimed credits, proposed allocation, and implementing transfer or sale. A general statement that all marital assets should be divided equitably is inadequate when specific accounts or transactions are disputed.

For a parenting issue, organize child-specific proof under the current governing framework, including safety and existing arrangements where relevant. Explain the actual schedule and exchanges requested, the effect on school and caregiving, and the response to the other parent’s proposal. Do not treat a temporary plan, civil restraint, or parenting professional’s recommendation as a final custody adjudication unless the record establishes it.

## Drafting example

Weak: “The home should be sold and the parties should share the equity equally.”

Acceptable approach: Address the disputed separate contribution, evidence of any claimed gift or tracing, valuation and payoff, sale expenses, occupancy, and supported credits. If a principal-reduction credit is sought, distinguish principal from interest, taxes, and insurance; identify the payment source and prevent crediting marital money as a separate contribution. State the sequence for paying verified obligations and allocating the remaining proceeds. Use only the formula authorized and supported in that matter.

## Acceptance checklist and handoff

- Each contested issue and material asset is analyzed, with supporting and adverse evidence and a precise requested determination.

- The brief distinguishes anticipated proof from actual testimony and proposed, marked, offered, and admitted exhibits.

- All relied-on sources are available or specifically flagged; all relief has an identified legal and factual basis.

- Financial formulas and alternatives avoid double counting and unexplained offsets; implementation is workable.

- The exhibit list and proof map support the arguments actually made. Co-counsel and “on the brief” credit are correct where authorized.

- Deliver the editable brief, issue and proof map, and a focused missing-evidence list. Include exhibit lists or authorities tables when within scope.

Reject the draft if it summarizes the parties’ positions without resolving the legal questions, omits a material asset, mischaracterizes an order, or claims an evidentiary status the trial record does not support.
