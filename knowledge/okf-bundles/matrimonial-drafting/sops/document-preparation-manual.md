# Document preparation SOP manual (version 1.1)

Tanya Freeman Law LLC

Developer implementation and attorney review edition

Prepared November 18, 2026 \| Version 1.1

This manual establishes the drafting process and acceptance standards for seven recurring work products and workflows: certifications, marital settlement agreements, letter briefs, trial briefs, trial exhibit lists, tables of authorities, and trial and motion exhibit assembly. Each SOP explains the required inputs, substantive work, document structure, quality checks, and handoff. The shared procedures apply to all seven.

The goal is a complete attorney-review draft that develops the client’s position from the actual record. A document is incomplete when it states a conclusion without applying the facts, omits a requested issue, or leaves the reviewer to reconstruct how the requested relief would work.

This is a firm drafting and implementation manual, not a statement that every listed practice is a court requirement. Court rules, signed orders, and matter-specific directions must be checked for the particular assignment. Proposed implementation controls are identified as such. The supervising attorney retains responsibility for legal positions, concessions, client advice, and release of work product.

## How to use this manual

| **Section**                             | **Use**                                                                                          |
|-----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Shared standards                        | Set the assignment, read and reconcile sources, and establish the quality threshold.             |
| SOP 1 Certifications                    | Convert the client’s account and supporting record into a coherent factual certification.        |
| SOP 2 Marital settlement agreements     | Translate approved terms into complete, workable obligations without changing the bargain.       |
| SOP 3 Letter briefs                     | Present focused legal advocacy supported by the motion record.                                   |
| SOP 4 Trial briefs                      | Organize each contested issue around the governing standard, proof, and requested determination. |
| SOP 5 Trial exhibit lists               | Connect every listed document to an issue, purpose, and evidentiary plan.                        |
| SOP 6 Tables of authorities             | Build an accurate index of the authorities actually cited, using the final document’s pages.     |
| SOP 7 Trial and motion exhibit assembly | Bates-stamp trial evidence pages; organize motion exhibits with numbered tabs and an index.      |
| Developer evaluation and reusable forms | Test the workflow against representative assignments and record unresolved issues.               |

Use the applicable SOP together with the shared standards. A task limited to proofreading, redlining, or a particular issue does not authorize a wholesale substantive rewrite. For a full preparation assignment, completing only an outline or generic template does not satisfy the task.

## Basis and maintenance

Version 1.1 adds SOP 7 and the corresponding evaluation tests, implementing the firm’s distinction between Bates-stamped trial evidence and motion exhibits organized with numbered tabs and an index.

This manual draws from Tanya L. Freeman’s recurring drafting directions and representative prior work reviewed for this assignment, including MSA review notes, a strengthened pendente lite letter brief, and selected trial-brief and alimony-letter-brief sections. The certifications, exhibit, and authority workflows also reflect the project directions and corrections available in the working history. This is not a claim that every historical file or every final document was independently reexamined. The reusable examples above omit client-specific identifiers and do not supply facts for any matter.

The firm’s recurring standards include complete first-person certifications, preservation of material facts, continuous numbering, faithful implementation of negotiated terms, substantive application of law, evidence tied to specific arguments, and careful adherence to formatting and scope. The evaluation method, review forms, and workflow controls are proposed implementation specifications developed from those standards; they are not represented as a previously adopted firm policy.

Legal reference points for maintenance include the New Jersey Judiciary’s January 2024 Preliminary Guidelines on the Use of Artificial Intelligence by New Jersey Lawyers and its March 30, 2026 notice encouraging internal AI policies with a sample starter template. They support ongoing attention to accuracy, confidentiality, supervision, and responsible use. The firm should check current guidance and its own approved technology policy before deployment. The detailed drafting procedures in this manual are firm practice recommendations rather than quotations of those notices.
