# SOP 1: Certifications

## Purpose and required result

Prepare a complete, credible account in the certifying person’s voice that supplies the facts needed for the requested relief or opposition. The certification should explain what happened, why the present application is necessary, and how the evidence supports the requested result. It should read as a client’s factual account prepared with counsel, rather than a legal brief written in the client’s name.

## Inputs and readiness

Obtain the current application and proposed relief, opposing papers where applicable, prior certifications, relevant orders and agreements, client account and corrections, financial statements and source records, proposed exhibits, and the applicable scheduling order. Confirm whether the assignment is an initial, opposing, cross-motion, reply, supplemental, or services certification. This SOP principally addresses party factual certifications; an attorney’s services certification requires its own fee records, factors, and applicable rule checks.

Confirm personal knowledge, represented party, permitted scope, filing status, page cap, whether attachments count, and required exhibit treatment. A 20- or 25-page instruction from another matter does not establish the cap for this one. Identify exact factual questions the client must answer; do not send a general request to “provide more detail” when the missing information is specific.

## Preparation procedure

1\. List every item of relief requested by either side. Create an allegation-response matrix for opposing and reply submissions, identifying the allegation, source paragraph, response, supporting evidence, and consequence for relief.

2\. Build a chronology and procedural summary from actual orders and filings. Explain how the current application arose. For post-judgment matters, identify the relevant agreement or order provision and the later conduct rather than repeating the entire divorce history.

3\. Organize the account under background and procedural history, current circumstances, issue-specific responses, and relief sought. Adjust the order so threshold questions and urgent facts are easy to find. Use subheadings with continuous paragraph numbers across the document.

4\. Draft in first person. Use “I,” “we,” “my former spouse,” or the approved relational labels where natural. Avoid third-person references to the certifying person as “Plaintiff,” “Defendant,” or “the party” throughout the narrative.

5\. For each material event, state the date or supported period, actor, conduct, basis of knowledge, effect, and relevance. Explain concrete financial or parenting consequences. Identify the source of information that was not personally observed and flag hearsay or foundation concerns for counsel.

6\. Present accusations as allegations unless admitted or judicially determined. Convert emotional descriptions into specific events without stripping the client’s experience from the narrative. Do not invent motive, diagnosis, intent, or a causal link the evidence does not establish.

7\. Introduce exhibits where they support the account. Verify the actual document, date, completeness, and referenced passage. Maintain a separate exhibit ledger and ensure every reference matches the attached exhibit. If told to use all client exhibits, map all of them and flag any apparently irrelevant, privileged, or duplicative item for counsel rather than silently omitting it.

8\. Separate facts from legal argument. A party may explain why an event matters and what relief is requested, but detailed case analysis belongs in the brief. Preserve internal source mapping when visible record citations are not wanted.

9\. End with specific relief that matches the application and proposed order. Include the required dated certification language and signature treatment using the current approved form and applicable rule, including Rule 1:4-4(b) where applicable. Never insert a signature, adoption of facts, or execution date without authority.

10\. Reconcile the client’s revisions against supporting documents and the prior draft. Maintain a targeted question list, verify figures and calculations, render the document, and enforce the actual page cap without reducing required typography.

## Structure and issue development

| **Component**         | **Required substance**                                                                                                |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Introduction          | Identify the certifier, role, application, and factual purpose.                                                       |
| Background            | Essential relationship, children if relevant, controlling orders, and procedural events.                              |
| Current circumstances | Present income, expenses, parenting arrangements, compliance, or other conditions relevant to the relief.             |
| Responses             | Answer each material opposing allegation with specific facts, evidence, and necessary context.                        |
| Requested relief      | Explain the factual basis for each requested outcome, including primary and alternative relief authorized by counsel. |
| Execution             | Correct certification statement, date, and signature block; no unsupported execution details.                         |

In a support application, connect the budget to actual payment sources, unpaid expenses, available funds, and present need. In enforcement, quote or accurately identify the operative obligation, describe compliance and breach, and reconcile claimed arrears. In modification, identify the asserted change and its timing. In parenting disputes, describe caregiving, logistics, safety, and child-specific effects without turning disputed allegations into findings.

## Drafting example

Weak: “My spouse controls all the money and refuses to support me.”

Acceptable approach: State when the regular deposit stopped, which account historically paid household bills, what payment requests followed, what remains unpaid, and the supporting records. Distinguish a redirected deposit from money proven to have been spent or dissipated. Use a figure only when supported. The paragraph should explain the resulting need and the requested payment arrangement.

## Acceptance checklist and handoff

- The narrative uses first person, meaningful paragraphs, clear subheadings, and continuous automatic numbering.

- Every requested item of relief and material opposing allegation has been addressed or specifically flagged.

- Dates, amounts, knowledge, and exhibit descriptions match the sources; the narrative does not overstate findings.

- The certification, financial schedules, brief, and proposed order agree on operative facts and requested amounts.

- The actual rendered document meets the assigned page cap and typography; exhibits are counted according to the applicable instruction.

- Deliver the Word draft, exhibit index if within scope, and a separate factual confirmation list. Identify signature and missing-proof items before release.

Reject the draft if it replaces fact development with conclusions, adopts counsel’s legal argument as the client’s personal knowledge, fabricates an exhibit, or fails to answer a central allegation. A truthful one-sentence answer does not justify invented elaboration; combine related supported facts where appropriate.
