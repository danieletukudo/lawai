# SOP 6: Tables of authorities

## Purpose and required result

Produce a table of authorities (TOA) that accurately indexes the legal authorities actually cited in the final brief. Its references point to pages of that brief, not to reporter pinpoint pages. The table must remain synchronized with the brief through final pagination and export.

## Inputs and readiness

Obtain the exact current brief in editable form, any required filing format, court instructions, citation style, and the intended final rendered document. Identify whether the TOA is integrated into the brief or delivered separately, how front matter is paginated, and which page labels the final filing uses.

Determine whether a TOA is required or requested for this submission. Do not apply an appellate requirement automatically to a Family Part letter brief or trial brief. A TOA assignment does not authorize adding new legal argument or researching new authorities unless requested.

## Preparation procedure

1\. Freeze a named working version of the brief. Inspect body text, footnotes, tables, captions, and other locations where legal authorities appear. Read the actual document, not just a prior citation list.

2\. Build an authority occurrence register. For each citation, record the authority identity, category, full citation, exact occurrence, short-form variants, and context needed to resolve “Id.” or “supra.”

3\. Normalize different references to the same authority. Merge full and short forms only after confirming identity. Keep distinct opinions in the same litigation separate when the citations identify different decisions.

4\. Validate citations against the approved research record. Flag incomplete names, reporter volumes, courts, dates, publication status, and pinpoints. A TOA can expose a citation defect; it cannot validate the legal proposition by listing the case.

5\. Group authorities in the required order. A conventional structure uses cases, statutes, rules, and other authorities. Alphabetize cases and sort statutes and rules consistently under the applicable style. Include constitutional provisions or other categories where used.

6\. Generate the table through a supported Word TOA workflow or another reproducible method. If using fields, verify citation categories and full/short forms; do not assume inserting a field also creates a correct displayed table.

7\. Insert the TOA and any contents pages before final pagination. Render the complete brief with all front matter. Derive every reference from the actual final brief page on which the authority appears, including footnote citations.

8\. Check both directions: every cited authority appears in the TOA under the appropriate identity, and every TOA entry appears in the brief. Deduplicate repeated references on the same page. Review ambiguous short forms manually.

9\. Compare the table’s page references with the final rendered brief. Check any permitted “passim” usage against the court’s convention; do not use it to avoid accurate page mapping. An entry’s reporter page or section number must never be mistaken for a brief page.

10\. After any edit that may change pagination or authorities, rebuild or refresh, repaginate, and check again. If the runtime cannot reliably update Word TOA fields, do not label the result final; identify the needed update in the intended Word environment and verify the resulting filing copy.

## Required distinctions

| **Item**            | **Correct treatment**                                                                                     |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Reporter citation   | Identifies the decision, such as its reporter volume and opening page.                                    |
| Reporter pinpoint   | Identifies the passage within the authority supporting the proposition.                                   |
| TOA page reference  | Identifies where the authority is cited in this brief.                                                    |
| Short form          | Must resolve to the correct full authority before consolidation.                                          |
| Printed brief page  | Use the final filing’s pagination convention, including Roman numeral front matter where applicable.      |
| PDF viewer position | Do not substitute the viewer’s sequential page number when the filing uses different printed page labels. |

## Drafting example

If a case appears by full citation on brief page 4, by a short form on page 7, and in a footnote on page 9, the entry must account for pages 4, 7, and 9. A reporter pinpoint of 306–07 remains part of the brief’s citation; it is not a TOA reference to pages 306 and 307 of the brief. If adding the TOA moves the argument, the page references must be regenerated.

## Acceptance checklist and handoff

- All cited authorities, including footnotes, have been inventoried and normalized correctly.

- Full citations are complete or the precise defect is flagged; short forms do not merge unrelated decisions.

- The TOA contains no uncited authorities and omits no cited authority within its intended scope.

- References match the final complete rendered brief’s page labels, not a prior draft or the reporter pages.

- Field behavior and display have been tested in the intended editing workflow; any static table is clearly identified as requiring regeneration after edits.

- Deliver the integrated Word document when that is the assignment, or the requested separate table, with the source brief version and verification status identified internally.

Reject the draft if citations are collected without page checking, the table predates the final layout, authorities are invented to fill gaps, or a static table is represented as automatically updating.
